Dwindling “I love you” and positive affirmation posts

I've been struggling to find posts online that genuinely offer positive affirmations or even the words “I love you” in them.

I've recently been struggling to find posts online that genuinely offer positive affirmations or even the words "I love you" in them. I struggle with making human connections so spend most of my time online. Which can be lonely a lot of times so when I stumble upon a post that speaks positively, especially about mental health and the struggles that come with having mental health problems, it helps me get through just one more day. These posts seem to be few and far between lately which makes me think that maybe other people struggling with mental health don't believe in such posts anymore, especially hearing "I love you" from a random stranger(which might sound like sympathy )OR the world and all that's happening inside it has destroyed people's sense of empathy and compassion. It's just sad to see so many exaggerated posts on a daily basis rather than caring one's. The world is already trying to tear us down every chance it gets, would it really hurt if someone just took the time to show care (or claim care because they don't personally know you but understand how hard it is to keep going most times) Maybe it's just me. I would appreciate different perspectives on this, so please let me know what you think or how you feel about "I love you" and positive affirmation posts. Do they sound and feel disingenuous?

Can stress cause Bell's palsy?

Bell's palsy is a sudden weakness or drooping on one side of the face. Many people ask whether stress is to blame. Stress may play a part for some people, but doctors usually look at other explanations first, and in most cases the exact cause is never confirmed.

The condition is thought to happen when the nerve that moves the facial muscles becomes inflamed and swollen. That nerve is squeezed as it passes through a narrow channel in the skull, which is why even a small amount of swelling can affect how the face moves.

Viral infections are a common suspect. The virus behind cold sores, and the one that causes chickenpox and later shingles, can lie dormant in the body and flare up again, sometimes inflaming this nerve.

Poorly controlled diabetes can also damage nerves, including the facial nerve, and produce symptoms that look like Bell's palsy. Lyme disease is a rarer cause.

Because a drooping face can also be a sign of a stroke, always get medical help quickly when it appears suddenly. A health professional can find the cause, start treatment early and talk with you about anything that may be adding to your stress.
